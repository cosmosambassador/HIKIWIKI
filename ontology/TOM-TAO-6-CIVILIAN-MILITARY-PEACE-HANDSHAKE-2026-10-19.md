## Founder position
TJ Morris proposes that the Tom Tao 6 Payload Bridge coordinate civilian and military computing domains around human dignity, accountable use, and peace. The intent is to prioritize nonviolent, beneficial applications of advanced computation, robotics, drones, and AI-enabled systems. This is a normative position, not a claim that existing defense systems have adopted Tom Tao 6.

## Technical clarification
Supercomputing is not governed by one exclusive governmental programming language. Civilian and military high-performance computing use many programming languages, hardware architectures, networks, and security controls. “AI” and “SI” are not inherently incompatible technologies; terminology and capabilities must be defined separately.

## Peace handshake — proposed safeguards
1. **Identify:** authenticated originator, destination, and purpose.
2. **Classify:** civilian, dual-use, restricted, or military context; protect classified and private data.
3. **Authorize:** explicit accountable human approval, with separation of duties for consequential actions.
4. **Constrain:** deny unsafe or unauthorized actions; require compliance with applicable law, human rights, and international humanitarian law.
5. **Audit:** immutable or tamper-evident provenance and independent review.
6. **Confirm:** verify safe receipt, accessibility, and intended non-harmful result.

A handshake cannot itself guarantee peace. Governance, institutions, law, verification, and meaningful human control are necessary.
