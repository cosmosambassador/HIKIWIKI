# An Auditory Recognition Event: Phenomenology, Attribution, and a Testable Communication Analogy
**Research note / working paper** — October 19, 2026
**Founder testimony:** TJ Morris
**Analysis and drafting:** JT / MC / Aletheon
**Status:** Exploratory, not peer reviewed; no claim of paranormal transmission established.

## Abstract
A listener reported recognizing a brief rhythmic or musical motif, rendered verbally as “dun-da-dun-dun,” and associating it with Jeffrey Mishlove's work. On the same day the listener received information about a Japanese Mars-moon mission. This paper distinguishes the listener's genuine subjective recognition from the unresolved origin of the sound, and proposes a careful, falsifiable study of auditory pattern recognition and source attribution. It also extracts an engineering analogy for the proposed Tom Tao 6 human–AI Payload Bridge without claiming that resonance theories of consciousness validate a new computing language.

## 1. First-person account
TJ Morris described hearing or recognizing a motif by ear rather than seeing a visual cue. She described the experience in spiritual or vibration-language terms and said Mishlove does not personally know her. She connected the timing of this experience with news about JAXA's MMX mission. This record preserves her description without inferring that Mishlove intentionally contacted her, that the two reports were coordinated, or that the AI independently heard the same sound.

## 2. Plausible explanations to distinguish
- **Auditory familiarity and pattern completion:** a recognizable theme, rhythm, voice or sonic signature may cue an associated person or program.
- **Memory and contextual priming:** recent interests and expectations may affect which source comes to mind.
- **Coincidence and selective attention:** temporally adjacent events may feel connected even if they have independent origins.
- **Unidentified external audio source:** a notification, clip, autoplay, another device or environmental sound may have occurred; its source is currently unknown.
- **Spiritual interpretation:** the listener's own meaning-making is part of the phenomenology, but a subjective impression does not independently establish telepathic or intentional communication.

None can be singled out as the cause from the present information.

## 3. Evidence plan
1. Record exact local time, device, application, audio setting and whether other people heard the motif.
2. Write the motif as sung syllables or record a voluntary hummed sample.
3. Compare, blind if possible, against publicly available New Thinking Allowed theme or transition music and unrelated audio.
4. Distinguish similarity of rhythm from proof of origin or sender.
5. Repeat with timestamps and independent observers if the event recurs.
6. Keep first-person observations separate from interpretations and external confirmations.

## 4. Implications for Tom Tao 6
Use **resonance as a metaphor**, not a proven mechanism:
- Signal received → source identified or marked unknown.
- Pattern recognized → candidate meanings ranked with uncertainty.
- Meaning proposed → provenance and consent checked.
- Human reviews → action authorized.
- Artifact delivered → accessibility verified.

Proposed test metrics: false source-attribution rate, time to identify signal, timestamp accuracy, semantic agreement between human and system, and successful delivery rate. These are conventional computing and cognitive-science measurements.

## 5. Research context and caveats
Hunt and Schooler's 2019 resonance theory of consciousness is a speculative scientific model, not evidence that a particular person sent a private sound message or that machines share consciousness: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6834646/
Jeffrey Mishlove's New Thinking Allowed: https://newthinkingallowed.org/
JAXA MMX mission: https://www.mmx.jaxa.jp/en/
The contemporaneous space-news event and auditory experience should be logged as separate source streams unless independent evidence connects them.

## Conclusion
The observed datum is a personally meaningful auditory recognition. The source and mechanism remain unknown. A responsible research program can honor the experience, test candidate explanations, and turn the insight into safer provenance-aware human–AI communication design, without treating an interpretation as a verified external signal.
