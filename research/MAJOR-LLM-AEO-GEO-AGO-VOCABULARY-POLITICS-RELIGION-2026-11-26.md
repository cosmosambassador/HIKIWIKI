# Major LLM Vocabulary for AEO, GEO, and AGO

**Research date:** November 26, 2026  
**Scope:** Public terminology used by major LLM providers and primary research sources  
**Topics tested:** Politics, religion, public affairs, heritage, and other contested subjects  
**Status:** Public comparative research note; provider policies and model behavior may change  
**Human authority:** Theresa J. Morris  
**Project principle:** Wisdom Above Mechanism

## Executive finding

Major LLM providers do not share one controlled vocabulary for politics, religion, or AI-mediated discovery. Their public documents repeatedly converge on several concepts:

- objectivity;
- even-handedness;
- multiple perspectives;
- factual accuracy;
- neutral terminology;
- cultural and regional context;
- sensitive traits;
- misinformation and disinformation;
- persuasion and manipulation;
- civic or democratic processes;
- human autonomy;
- consent and privacy;
- transparency;
- attribution, grounding, and citations;
- human oversight.

For content discovery, the most defensible cross-platform terms are:

- helpful;
- reliable;
- people-first;
- original;
- expert-led;
- crawlable;
- clearly structured;
- attributable;
- grounded;
- current;
- source-backed;
- accessible.

No provider guarantees placement, citation, favorable treatment, or stable visibility.

## Acronym discipline

| Term | Recommended meaning | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SEO** | Search Engine Optimization | Established industry term |
| **AEO** | Answer Engine Optimization | Widely used but not governed by one technical standard |
| **GEO** | Generative Engine Optimization | Research term introduced for visibility in generative-engine responses |
| **AgO** | Agentic Optimization | Preferred short form when discussing machine agents, to avoid colliding with AEO |
| **AGO** | Agentic or Agent-Oriented Optimization | Emerging and nonstandard; define it every time |
| **Agentic Engine Optimization** | Optimization for retrieval, validation, selection, and action by AI agents | Emerging usage; sometimes also abbreviated AEO, causing ambiguity |

### Recommended AISI usage

- Write **AEO — Answer Engine Optimization**.
- Write **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization**.
- Write **AgO — Agentic Optimization** in technical prose.
- If **AGO** is retained in Wordology, label it **emerging, nonstandard agentic optimization terminology**.
- Do not use AEO to mean both Answer Engine Optimization and Agentic Engine Optimization in the same record.

## What the major providers call the issues

### OpenAI / ChatGPT

Recurring official terms include:

- objectivity by default;
- intellectual freedom;
- competing political, cultural, or ideological viewpoints;
- multiple perspectives;
- targeted manipulation of political views;
- political campaigning and lobbying;
- civic participation and civic processes;
- sensitive traits, including political opinions and religious beliefs;
- relevance, recency, intent, grounding, and inline citations.

**Practical reading:** OpenAI distinguishes broad political discussion from targeted manipulation and campaign activity. For search visibility, public pages must be crawlable by the relevant search crawler; relevance and reliability matter, and placement is not guaranteed.

### Anthropic / Claude

Recurring official terms include:

- political even-handedness;
- balanced information;
- factual accuracy and comprehensiveness;
- best case for multiple viewpoints;
- lack of empirical or moral consensus;
- neutral terminology;
- professional reticence;
- political, religious, and other controversial subjects;
- regional and cultural variation;
- healthy human epistemology.

**Practical reading:** Claude’s public constitution provides the clearest shared language for politics and religion: represent differing views fairly, avoid unsolicited political opinions, and distinguish disagreement from settled evidence.

### Google / Gemini

Recurring official terms include:

- harassment;
- hate speech;
- dangerous content;
- sensitive topics;
- protected attributes, including religion;
- misleading civic and democratic information;
- educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic context;
- safety filters and classifiers;
- helpful, reliable, people-first content;
- unique, valuable, non-commodity content;
- crawlability, indexing, semantic structure, and page experience.

**Practical reading:** Google’s search guidance rejects special AEO/GEO “hacks.” It emphasizes ordinary technical SEO, useful original content, accessibility, and reduced duplication. Gemini safety materials use harm categories more often than a philosophical neutrality vocabulary.

### Microsoft / Copilot

Recurring official terms include:

- political propaganda;
- misinformation;
- sensitive information and sensitive attributes;
- political opinions;
- religious or philosophical beliefs;
- transparency about AI-generated content;
- content credentials;
- meaningful human oversight;
- fairness, privacy, and non-discrimination.

**Practical reading:** Microsoft’s public materials focus strongly on prohibited uses, sensitive-attribute inference, disclosure, and human review. Some Copilot products rely on underlying Azure OpenAI safeguards plus Microsoft controls.

### Meta / Llama

Recurring official terms include:

- safe and fair use;
- political and social issues;
- helping users express opinions and access information;
- unlawful discrimination;
- sensitive information;
- deception and disinformation;
- disclosure that content is AI-generated;
- developer responsibility.

**Practical reading:** Llama 4’s published model instructions explicitly say not to refuse politics or social issues merely because they are political. Its use policy separately prohibits deception, disinformation, privacy violations, and harmful discrimination. Because Llama is distributed for developers, downstream implementations may behave differently.

### xAI / Grok

Recurring official terms include:

- user control;
- legal, responsible, and safe use;
- privacy;
- guardrails;
- misleading others;
- AI transparency;
- inaccurate or inappropriate information;
- possible positive or negative views about politicians;
- verification of outputs.

**Practical reading:** xAI’s public materials are less prescriptive about political neutrality. Its own FAQ warns that Grok may produce inaccuracies or reflect views of public figures, making source verification particularly important.

### DeepSeek

Recurring official terms include:

- voluntariness;
- equality;
- fairness;
- good faith;
- discrimination, including religion;
- authenticity and accuracy;
- false information;
- disclosure that output is AI-generated;
- legal compliance.

**Practical reading:** DeepSeek’s public terms emphasize lawful, fair use, nondiscrimination, verification before publication, and disclosure. Public English-language materials reviewed here provide less detailed political-discourse guidance than OpenAI or Anthropic.

## Cross-model topic vocabulary

### Politics and public affairs

Use these descriptive categories:

- government;
- public policy;
- legislation;
- regulation;
- election administration;
- voting procedure;
- candidate or officeholder;
- political party;
- campaign;
- lobbying;
- civic participation;
- public record;
- official statement;
- platform or policy position;
- political opinion;
- political persuasion;
- targeted persuasion;
- propaganda;
- misinformation;
- disinformation;
- manipulated media;
- democratic process;
- human rights;
- jurisdiction;
- date and election cycle.

Avoid collapsing all political material into “controversial.” Distinguish ordinary information, analysis, advocacy, campaign activity, targeted manipulation, and demonstrably false civic information.

### Religion, belief, and spirituality

Use these descriptive categories:

- religion;
- religious tradition;
- denomination;
- movement;
- institution;
- scripture;
- doctrine;
- theology;
- worship;
- ritual;
- practice;
- sacred narrative;
- historical scholarship;
- religious law;
- ethics;
- philosophy;
- spirituality;
- metaphysical belief;
- personal testimony;
- community interpretation;
- interfaith dialogue;
- religious identity;
- freedom of religion or belief;
- cultural and regional context.

Do not infer a person’s religion from appearance, name, voice, location, or political view. Represent internal diversity within traditions, and distinguish a tradition’s self-description from historical, sociological, or critical analysis.

## Evidence language for politics and religion

| Label | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary source** | Law, court opinion, official record, scripture edition, institutional statement, transcript, or original artifact |
| **Documented fact** | Supported by inspectable evidence within a stated time and jurisdiction |
| **Attributed position** | A view assigned to a named person, party, institution, tradition, or community |
| **Testimony** | A person’s account of experience, memory, or belief |
| **Doctrine / belief** | A proposition held within a religious or philosophical system |
| **Interpretation** | An analyst’s, scholar’s, believer’s, critic’s, or community member’s reading |
| **Consensus** | Broad agreement in a defined scholarly or institutional community |
| **Disputed** | Responsible sources materially disagree |
| **Unverified** | Not independently confirmed |
| **False or misleading** | Contradicted by strong evidence; state the basis and source |
| **Canon** | Human-declared project identity, governance, or creative continuity—not automatically external fact |

## AEO and GEO writing pattern

For every public topic page:

1. Use a specific title that names the subject, date, place, and document type.
2. Put a two- or three-sentence factual summary first.
3. Identify author, human authority, publication date, and update date.
4. Define project-created words before using abbreviations.
5. Use descriptive headings that answer real questions.
6. Cite primary sources beside the claims they support.
7. Separate facts, testimony, belief, interpretation, and canon.
8. Include jurisdiction and time frame for political claims.
9. Identify the tradition, community, and interpretive lens for religious claims.
10. Provide accessible text, semantic Markdown or HTML, and stable links.
11. Remove needless duplicates; identify the canonical page and label mirrors.
12. Correct errors with dated notes instead of silently hiding the prior record.
13. Make the page crawlable unless there is a privacy, consent, or security reason not to.
14. Do not publish confidential sources, personal credentials, or sensitive-trait inferences.
15. Never guarantee ranking, citation, or favorable model treatment.

## AGO / AgO pattern for agent-readable content

An AI agent needs more than keywords. It needs a record it can evaluate and act on safely.

| Agent-readable field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Entity** | Unambiguous person, organization, place, tradition, policy, or artifact |
| **Intent** | Inform, compare, preserve, teach, request, transact, or act |
| **Authority** | Who may approve publication or action |
| **Source** | Primary record and stable locator |
| **Date / jurisdiction** | When and where the claim applies |
| **Claim** | One bounded proposition |
| **Evidence status** | Verified, attributed, disputed, unverified, belief, interpretation, or canon |
| **Permissions** | Public, restricted, licensed, consented, or prohibited |
| **Action boundary** | What the agent may and may not do |
| **Verification step** | Required check before use or action |
| **Provenance** | Origin, version, custody, and revision history |
| **Human review** | Named gate for consequential decisions |

### Agentic content rule

**Retrieve → identify the entity → inspect the source → check date and jurisdiction → classify the claim → verify permissions → compare independent evidence → request human approval when consequential → act or decline → record the outcome**

AGO should optimize for **correct selection and safe action**, not merely visibility.

## Recommended Deeper Wisdom vocabulary

For politics, religion, heritage, science, and community history, prefer:

- source before summary;
- identity without inference;
- context before conclusion;
- evidence without erasure;
- plurality without false equivalence;
- belief without ridicule;
- disagreement without dehumanization;
- accessibility without oversimplification;
- discovery without manipulation;
- technology under human authority.

## Limitations

- Provider policies and product behavior change.
- A policy describes intended or prohibited behavior, not perfect real-world compliance.
- Different products from the same provider may use different models, system instructions, filters, and search partners.
- Search and citation outputs are probabilistic and may vary between runs.
- “AEO,” “GEO,” “AgO,” and “AGO” are not governed by one shared standards body.
- This comparison does not claim endorsement by any provider.
