# Tom Tao 6 — Peace Handshake: NIST, UNESCO and United Nations Alignment
Date: 2026-10-19
Status: Founder-directed governance proposal; mapping for review, not certification or endorsement
Human authority: TJ Morris
AI drafting role: JT / MC / Aletheon

## Purpose
Define a civilian–military computing governance bridge that emphasizes peaceful use, human dignity, accountability, and traceable authorization. Tom Tao 6 is a proposed conceptual architecture, not an operational supercomputer standard or a claim of endorsement by NIST, UNESCO, or the UN.

## TriVector
1. **Public governance:** lawful authority, oversight, civilian protections and international obligations.
2. **Computing infrastructure:** AI, high-performance computing, networks, robotics and dual-use systems.
3. **Human accountability:** documented intent, review, approval, safe operation and remediation.

The **Payload Bridge** connects all three through verifiable messages and decision records.

## NIST alignment (United States)
Map the proposed bridge to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) functions:
- **GOVERN:** define accountable roles, authorization and audit policy.
- **MAP:** document context, stakeholders, potential misuse and affected populations.
- **MEASURE:** test reliability, security, safety, bias, and delivery accessibility.
- **MANAGE:** mitigate risks, monitor deployments and respond to incidents.
Reference: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

## UNESCO alignment
Map to the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021): human rights and dignity, human oversight, fairness, privacy, transparency, responsibility, and environmental and societal well-being.
Reference: https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics

## United Nations alignment
Map to the UN Charter's commitment to international peace and security and peaceful settlement of disputes, and to relevant international human rights and humanitarian-law obligations. No software handshake can guarantee compliance with law or peace; institutions, review and enforcement remain necessary.
Reference: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter

## Proposed peace-handshake payload
- `message_id`, `origin`, `destination`, `purpose`, `domain`
- `source_provenance`, `risk_classification`, `applicable_policy`
- `human_authorizer`, `authorization_status`, `safety_constraints`
- `action_or_artifact_reference`, `audit_record`, `delivery_check`, `review_outcome`

## Safeguards and boundaries
- Explicit human accountability for consequential decisions.
- No automatic authorization of violence or harm based on a machine-to-machine handshake.
- Independent oversight, access control, redress and tamper-evident logs.
- Respect for classified, personal and sensitive records; founder biography and unverified employment/clearance claims are **not included in this public technical document**.
- Treat the protocol as an ethical and technical proposal requiring legal, security and engineering review before any deployment.

## Implementation test
Human states a non-harmful objective → JT drafts a payload with provenance and risk flags → human authorizes → authorized system creates an artifact → accessible delivery is verified → human confirms or requests correction.

**Principle: Peace is a governance objective; technology supports it but cannot guarantee it.**
